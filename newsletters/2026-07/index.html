Newsletter Highlights

 👀 Did you receive our June newsletter?  
You probably didn't notice the mistake we made - but we did and we're setting the record straight here! 😅
 
In our "Replications gaining momentum" section, we accidentally named the wrong podcast. The feature should have credited Metascience Matters, hosted by Randy Ellis—a fantastic podcast covering research integrity, replication, peer review, research incentives, AI, and data reliability.

Lukas Röseler was featured in a recent interview exploring FORRT's replication ecosystem, the vision behind Replication Research (R2), the Replication Journal Federation, and Guess the Replication— an educational game that invites players to predict whether published findings successfully replicated.

Thanks for bearing with us and our apologies to Randy Ellis for the mix-up!

Project Updates

 FORRT at SIPS 2026  
The Society for the Improvement of Psychological Science (SIPS) 2026 featured several exciting FORRT-related recognitions and contributions.

Most notably, Replication Research received one of the SIPS Mission Awards. We also spotted a number of FORRT-connected initiatives and presentations, including the ECR Reviewers Platform led by Rachel Heyard, Love Replications Week co-organized by FORRT, The Advantage of Big Team Science featuring several FORRT members, and Why I Declare a Conflict of Interest - and You Should Too by Steven Verheyen.

Congratulations to everyone helping advance open, rigorous, and collaborative science!

 Replication Hub Updates  
The Replication Hub continues to make replications more visible. As part of the Making Replications Count project, the team has now posted 99 PubPeer comments linking original studies to their replications—with over 2,400 more comments to follow.

FORRT at UNESCO's IDSSD Conference
FORRT was proud to attend the first International Decade of Science for Sustainable Development (IDSSD) Conference at UNESCO Headquarters in Paris. Representing FORRT were Sarah, Director of Social Justice & DEIA, and Meera, FORRT Steward.

The conference brought together ministers, leaders of international scientific organisations, and representatives of endorsed programmes from around the world.

A key message emerged: 
While we have the knowledge to tackle global challenges, the next step is turning that knowledge into policy - and policy into meaningful action.

This is a challenge FORRT is embracing through FORWARD, our IDSSD programme. Stay tuned for its official launch and the work ahead throughout the decade.

 Looking for Leads
 Open Research Across Disciplines
FORRT is now hosting Open Research Across Disciplines, a curated collection of open research resources originally developed by Emily Farran, Priya Silverstein, Camilla Gilmore, and colleagues, alongside the UK Reproducibility Network.

We're looking for discipline leads (individuals, teams, or organisations) to help review and update their field's page, either as a one-off contribution or an annual commitment. Contributors will be recognised on the project, with annual leads also listed on the FORRT and UKRN webpages.

📩 Interested? Contact Priya Silverstein via email or the FORRT Slack.
