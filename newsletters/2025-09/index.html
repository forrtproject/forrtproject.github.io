Welcome to FORRT's Newsletter!
With this newsletter, we hope to inform the larger community about FORRT's activities in the last months, and plans in the upcoming months. This digest is intended to help you cut through the noise and stay involved. If you missed our last newsletter, you can find it here.  

We'd love to hear your feedback, email info@forrt.org if you have thoughts or questions.

Newsletter Highlights
Want to help shape the future of FORRT? Join our new Steering Committee
At our recent FORRT Leadership Summit, we decided to create a new Steering Committee to guide our next chapter! We’re looking for nominations (self- or others) for people who can bring skills, ideas, and energy to running our community. We welcome all nominations, including early career scholars.

This could mean leading in areas like: 
🎯 Metascience 
📚 Education & Training 
📢 Advocacy & Outreach 
💬 Community Building 
💰 Fundraising 
🌈 DEIA in OS 
🤝 Partnerships 
⚙️ Operations 
Or something new! 

Nominate yourself or someone else here.
There’s just ONE short question: tell us who they are and what they could bring. We’ll work together to find the best fit!

Coming later this year: elections for representatives of FORRT community groups, from special interest areas to groups bringing underrepresented perspectives, to make sure our leadership reflects our whole community.

Let’s build this together!

Project Updates

New FORRT Paper:
"Incorporating Feminist Practices Into Psychological Science — 

the Why, the What, and the How"
Feminism isn’t just theory—it’s a powerful tool for transforming how we do science. This new perspective piece explores why feminist approaches matter in psychology, what they look like in practice, and how to start integrating them into your own research. Includes a checklist, glossary, and tips from 8 feminist ECRs.

🌱 Make your science more inclusive.

New from FORRT’s Team Citation Politics!
"In Pursuit of Citational Justice"
Our first preprint & toolkit is out! Learn what citational politics are, why they matter, and how to make your citation practices more equitable—step by step.

Check out our new FORRT webpage to find links to the manuscript, the toolkit and a walk-through video introduction to the toolkit as well as future plans and how to get involved!

New Open Access Resource!
Handbook for Reproduction and Replication Studies
A practical, open-access guide to help researchers carry out high-quality reproductions and replications across the social sciences. Aiming to support also researchers who want to submit their reproduction or replication study to the Replication Research Journal. 

🔗 Check out the handbook

🧪 What’s inside?
  • A summary of recent developments
  • Step-by-step guidance across the full research process
  • Field-specific considerations
  • Tips for publishing your report
We're also inviting feedback and new chapter contributions!

📩 Interested in contributing? Contact Lukas Röseler (lukas.roeseler@uni-muenster.de) or Lukas Wallrich (l.wallrich@bbk.ac.uk).
Open Science in the Developing World: A Collection of Practical Guides for Researchers in Developing Countries
Team Open Science in Developing Countries at FORRT have published the paper, Open Science in the Developing World: A Collection of Practical Guides for Researchers in Developing Countries. 

The paper maps the opportunities and barriers facing Global South researchers, and proposes a four-level guide to support gradual engagement with open science in developing countries.
