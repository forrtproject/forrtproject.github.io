Project Updates
 
Grant success & opportunity to boost the update of replication research as a post-doc or research assistant
Lukas Wallrich and Lukas Röseler, from Team Replication Hub, have just received a UKRI Metascience research grant, for their project: 'Making Replications Count: Identifying Barriers and Enhancing Impact with Innovative Dissemination Tools'.

For the project, they are recruiting a postdoctoral researcher to join Lukas W (l.wallrich@bbk.ac.uk) at Birkbeck, University of London. There is an option for visa sponsorship, so any PhD-holder is welcome to apply. They are also advertising for research assistants to work on this project with Lukas R at the Münster Center of Open Science (the applicants for this must be student at a German university).

Check out the UK job description here. Further details on the research assistant posts to follow. If you have any questions on this, please contact Lukas W and Lukas R.
 
Open Social Psychology textbook
Last month, we launched our new FORRT initiative, which aims to develop an open-access textbook on social psychology summarizing the classical findings and discuss them in light of replication attempts. The project started with 16 draft textbook chapters written by undergraduate students (credited as first authors), which needed to be reviewed and improved upon.

The project launch has been a huge success, with 14 out of 16 chapters adopted for editing, and one new chapter being developed. The project is still looking for people to adopt and edit two chapters though, which focus on:
  • Facial Feedback Hypothesis
  • Heat Priming of Hostile Perception
There may also be scope to add new chapters to this textbook. You can find more information on the project here, and if you have any questions you can contact Rima-Maria Rahal via rahal.rimamaria@gmail.com.

Please make sure to read the project’s guidelines here before making a contribution.

Open Science in the Developing World: A Collection of Practical Guides for Researchers in Developing Countries
There is a new preprint from Team Open Science in Developing Countries at FORRT. The open science movement has transformed the research landscape, but its impact has largely been confined to developed countries. Researchers from developing countries have called for a redesign of open science to better align with their unique contexts. Practical actions are warranted though to drive meaningful and inclusive change. The preprint proposes a four-level guide to support gradual engagement with open science in developing countries.
