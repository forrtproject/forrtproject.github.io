    "abstract": "Understanding and embracing the tenets of \u201copen science\u201d is garnering traction among researchers in behavioral medicine, including SBM\u2019s \u201cTask Force on Open Science\u201d. Open science is seen as a scientific movement aimed at increasing openness, integrity, and reproducibility of scholarly research. It calls upon behavioral medicine researchers to consider complex, and often provocative, questions related to data sharing, registration of research plans and methods, and transparency in publishing. At the same time, many researchers are unsure about how to adopt these practices, and are concerned about how to \u201cdo\u201d open science across commonly used behavioral medicine research designs, including longitudinal observational studies and experimental research. This panel, organized by the International Journal of Behavioral Medicine, aims to provide both practical knowledge about open science practice as well as useful discussion from multiple perspectives. Panelists will include behavioral medicine principal investigators, a graduate student, a publisher (from Springer Publications), an NIH Representative (National Cancer Institute), journal editors/associate editors, as well as a representative from the Center for Open Science. We will use \u201cSlido\u201d, a smartphone platform that allows the audience to ask and rate questions. The most highly rated questions will be asked of the panel. The moderator will also maintain a list of questions. Examples include: \u2022 Will open science change how I do research? \u2022 How will open science affect publishing for me? \u2022 Is the evidence base strong enough to begin to shift incentive structures toward rewarding and acknowledging open science practices? \u2022 How can we guard against public humiliation when studies are not replicated? \u2022 How can the time required for pre-registration and other open science practices be built into student research programs? \u2022 When communicating our research to lay people and the media, how can we convey the difference between a pre-registered and entirely transparent study, and studies that have not followed these practices? \u2022 How can we balance openly sharing resources and with protecting our ideas and intellectual property given that not all researchers engage in open science practices? \u2022 What are the special considerations of open science for observational research? Does open science mean I cannot conduct secondary analyses? \u2022 What does it mean to register a study beforehand with a journal or study registry? \u2022 ",
