The aim of the **FORRT Library of Reproduction and Replication Attempts (FLoRA)** is to track all attempts to repeatedly test published findings across the sciences and humanities. In contrast to the FORRT Replication Database (FReD), FLoRA does not contain statistical data. This allows FLoRA to include references from any field of research, and to grow much faster.

## Replications (different data)

Replications are studies that intentionally repeat prior research to test whether the original findings hold. To be included in FLoRA, a study must:

- Self-identify as a replication (e.g., "replication of Author (Year)") before reporting results. The replication must be one of the study's main goals.
- Identify specific target study/studies that it replicates
- Replicate a study or experiment, not just a single association or finding

**Replications** can range from close/direct (same methods, same population) to conceptual (testing the same hypothesis with different methods), as long as the above criteria are met. Replication outcomes are coded as Successful, Failed, or Mixed, based on how the replication authors characterise their results. In rare cases, they can also be coded as "Successful but statistically flawed" (e.g., if the original finding was an artifact) or as inconclusive.

**Please treat outcome labels only as a starting point**. They depend on how replication authors describe their results. A failed replication does not by itself show that the original finding was false or the original study flawed: a replication can also fail by chance, because of low power or problems in its own conduct, or because conditions, situations, or contexts differed.

## Reproductions (same data)

Reproductions are attempts to computationally verify whether reported results can be obtained from the original study's data. They can be computational reproductions (using the same code) or robustness reproductions (using different code). Reproductions are coded along two dimensions:

- Computational success: Were the original results obtained? (Computationally Successful, Computational Issues, Not checked)
- Robustness: Do results hold under reasonable alternative specifications? (Robust, Robustness Challenges, or Robustness Not Checked)

## Structure of FLoRA

1.  Link repetition (replication/reproduction) references to original references

2.  Provide a standardized outcome (e.g., success, failure) based on the repetition report

With this simple structure, FLoRA allows for tracking of replication rates across time, disciplines, academic journals, and more. FLoRA is used for a wide range of existing projects, such as the [Replication Atlas](https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/), [Zotero Plugin](https://forrt.org/flora-zotero/), or [Chromium Extension](https://forrt.org/chromium-extension/).

## Reuse Guidelines

FLoRA is the work of over two hundred paid and volunteer researchers over more than four years. Please cite the respective works when (re-)using them. If you expect to profit from this work, for example through a grant application based on these data, please consider reaching out to us to involve members of the community.
